“I bet it doesn’t matter how I got here.”

Big Yatch, over black. The first words of the film.

A crime thriller played at game speed

Crime thriller, sports drama, organized crime. Friendship, ambition, betrayal, and the cost of getting what you want. The story is told by a narrator who can’t be fully trusted, and the audience should fall for him before they see what he’s hiding.

Big Yatch

Referee. Narrator. The man everyone watches.

Money, charm, and a need to win. An injury ended his playing days, so he took over the whistle. He believes being needed is the same as being loved, and he wants his best friend back on his terms.

Guy B

The better player. The quiet one.

All the talent, none of the spotlight. A gambling sting cost him years, and Big Yatch’s offer looks like a way back. Then the calls stop adding up, and he has to choose between loyalty and his future.

The woman who got away

Elegant, still, and always watching. She’s a different person to every man in the room: a husband, a team owner, a cowboy, two old friends. She never explains herself, and the film never lets her.

How the game runs

  1. Act I: The game is already in trouble

    Four referees at a bar. Guy B says he’s done. A warning about a cousin, a drink refill, a rifle across the street, a shot, black.

  2. Act II: How the friends got here

    College fragments, two different downfalls, the reunion, and the first calls that don’t add up. The operation starts to come apart.

  3. Act III: Who really controls the game?

    Back to the bar with new eyes. A laugh, a betrayal, a call that never connects, and one honest call on the court that the crowd will never forget.
